Research vision

Humans are one solution to adaptive, compositional cognition — not necessarily the best experimental preparation for studying it.

[DRAFT — replace with your own voice] My work sits at the intersection of computational neuroscience and AI interpretability, organized around a single long-standing question: what are the computational primitives that let a system generalize flexibly to novel situations, and how do they combine? Early experimental psychology and fMRI work gave me the empirical instincts; a materials-engineering background gave me a taste for mechanism over description. Both point the same direction — toward empirical, data-grounded frameworks (in the spirit of Stefano Fusi's approach to representational geometry) rather than purely theoretical or purely behavioral accounts.

Increasingly, I test these questions in artificial systems as well as biological ones — not as an abandonment of neuroscience, but as an expansion of the experimental substrate. Large language models and trained RNNs offer full access to the underlying computation in a way biological circuits rarely do, making them a powerful complementary tool for asking the same questions about compositional generalization, representational geometry, and mechanistic structure.

CPRO-RNN

Core dissertation project — context-dependent representational organization for compositional generalization

RNNs trained on compositional tasks recover generalization to novel task combinations only when co-trained on their primitive sub-tasks — a co-training regime that is both necessary and sufficient in low-data settings. The resulting representations show a distinct clustering signature that aligns with Next-Primitive-Prediction-style accounts of compositional structure. Preliminary single-unit analyses suggest an ordered algorithmic sequence (attend → evaluate congruence → logical combination → motor integration → closure detection); this is flagged as a preliminary observation pending SAE / decoding follow-up, not a settled claim.

White-Box Universal Transformers

Exact, hand-derivable algorithmic solutions to compositional reasoning tasks

Building universal transformers whose weights implement provably exact algorithms for compositional tasks. Boolean expression evaluation has a complete, hand-derived formal proof of correctness. Modular arithmetic and ListOps show the same perfect-generalization behavioral signature under standard training — suggestive of, but not proof of, an equivalent exact algorithm in the trained (non-hand-derived) case. Currently extending the approach to SCAN, COGS, and PCFG, with the aim of providing ground-truth oracles for open questions about minimal generating sets of compositional operations.

MDTB-Actflow

Linking task representational geometry to functional connectivity

Used activity-flow modeling to show how task-representational structure across the cortex relates to underlying functional connectivity patterns, in the Multi-Domain Task Battery (MDTB) dataset — connecting representational-geometry accounts of task structure to network-level mechanisms.
